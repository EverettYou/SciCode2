# Doped SMG and superconductivity

**Summary**: Doping a bilayer SMG parent can produce strong interlayer spin-singlet pairing, motivating a correlation-driven route to superconductivity that can be tested against unbiased finite-size simulations.

**Sources**: [Lu et al. (2023)](../../papers/lu2023sfdsm/paper.pdf) [Guo et al. (2026)](../../papers/guo2026sidsm/paper.pdf) [Chang et al. (2023)](../../papers/chang2023fssmg/paper.pdf)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-22

---

## Current synthesis

The proposed bilayer-nickelate mechanism starts from an interlayer-coupled, featureless Mott/SMG state and predicts interlayer spin-singlet superconductivity on doping, including a BCS-BEC crossover as coupling changes [Lu et al. (2023), §Abstract](../../papers/lu2023sfdsm/paper.pdf#Abstract). Sign-problem-free QMC later reports superconducting structure-factor and stiffness evidence on a related bilayer model, with pairing enhanced by onsite repulsion in the studied regime [Guo et al. (2026), §Conclusion](../../papers/guo2026sidsm/paper.pdf#Conclusion).

The undoped Fermi-surface-SMG simulations provide the parent spectral context, including broadened spectra and Green-function-zero surfaces [Chang et al. (2023), §Abstract](../../papers/chang2023fssmg/paper.pdf#Abstract). Agreement with material phenomenology remains suggestive rather than a unique microscopic identification.

## Benchmark opportunity

Separate parent-state identification from pairing: first reproduce the undoped gap and zero-surface diagnostics, then compare pairing correlations, stiffness, and finite-size scaling over doping and interactions. Test whether onsite repulsion enhances pairing after matching density and parent-state distance, not only bare couplings.
