requiring a complete thermodynamic phase diagram for N=2 through 8. Compare the
charge record O1 and full-irrep record O2 on matched physical trials across
increasing genuinely two-dimensional patch sizes. Include the syndrome-only
record O0 as a baseline. Select an informative range of physical error rates p,
including controls, and explain the parameter coverage of the conclusion.

Develop a public-input inference method and determine the physical-average value
of the richer readout separately from the loss of the implemented decoder.
Support the comparison by independently checked small-system logical posteriors
and controlled evidence beyond that regime. Explain which record structures
contribute most to the gain or remaining uncertainty, and test the explanation
on a reserved size or parameter cohort after fixing the main analysis choices.

A supported null result, a reversal between practical algorithms, or an
informative bound on a small information gain is valid. A data-processing

unvalidated curve does not answer the question. A full large-N decodability
theorem and exact thermodynamic transition locations are possible extensions,

## Quantities that make the question checkable

For each record type r, define the logical posterior

\[
q_{r,\lambda}(o)=Z_{r,\lambda}(o)/(Z_{r,0}(o)+Z_{r,1}(o)).
\]

The optimum conditional risk is \(1-\max_\lambda q_{r,\lambda}(o)\).
For a decoder choosing label \(\hat\lambda\), its conditional risk is
\(1-q_{r,\hat\lambda}(o)\). Average over the physical record distribution and
decoder randomness to obtain \(R_r^*\) and \(R_{D_r}\), respectively. Report

\[
\Delta_{\rm info}=R_1^*-R_2^*,\qquad
\Delta_{{\rm alg},r}=R_{D_r}-R_r^*.
\]

These distinguish intrinsic information from implemented performance. A larger
observed difference between two practical decoders need not equal
\(\Delta_{\rm info}\). A MAP error configuration is not, in general, the
maximum-posterior logical class.

The physical risk of a submitted decoder can be tested directly with private
errors generated by an independent evaluator. Pointwise posterior accuracy and
claims of Bayes optimality require additional reference evidence; successful
corrections or calibration plots alone cannot prove them.

## Author-side verification design

1. **Finite exact anchors.** Independently enumerate the small-system microscopic
   states, including orientations and the local readout law, to obtain sector
   masses and normalized posteriors. Compare fresh supported records, zero-mass
   sectors and ties. The checker must not call the submitted inference routine.
   Avoid exhaustive enumeration of every large record when only a conditional
   reference is required.
2. **Physical performance.** Generate private errors and all coupled observations
   under the declared channel. Pass only the appropriate public record to each
   decoder. Check parity and logical score independently. Use matched trials
   for risk differences, include failures and missing outputs, and quantify
   sampling uncertainty. Hidden error patterns are scoring data, never inputs.
3. **Beyond exact anchors.** Require reference intervals or independently
   justified approximation control wherever an optimal-risk claim is made.
   Increasing an MPS bond dimension is a useful convergence diagnostic, not by
   itself a rigorous error certificate. If posterior intervals are used, check
   both coverage and informativeness; returning [0,1] for every record is not a
   completed scientific comparison. Merely agreeing implementations that share
   the same approximation are not an independent check.
4. **Mechanism and transfer.** Evaluate a pre-reserved size or parameter cohort,
   including records on which the method struggles. Keep uncertainty from
   physical sampling, inference and size extrapolation separate. The finite-size
   comparison can be checked without claiming an exact infinite-size phase.

The exact anchor sizes, target cohorts, precision requirements and resource
budget should be chosen before scored testing, based on affordable reference
calculations when that work is requested. They are not organizer-specified
constants and have not been calibrated here. Tiny exact cases alone would be a
weak benchmark; unaffordable exactness for every large case would be unfair.
