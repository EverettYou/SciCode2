# Quantum error-correction decoding on D-Wave: formulation and embedding

Develop and validate a reusable way to translate check-matrix-defined syndrome decoding into a quadratic annealing model and embed it on ideal D-Wave Advantage2 Zephyr connectivity. Determine which formulation and embedding choices give a useful tradeoff between physical resource use, usable coefficient scale and classical compilation cost. QPU access is not required.

The accompanying `inputs/README.md`, `inputs/seed-codes.json` and `inputs/target-family.json` define the input conventions and two starting CSS examples. Your method must accept CSS check matrices rather than only these examples. Include the periodic toric family and a non-toric CSS family, and choose tractable sizes sufficient to support the comparisons you claim. Choose and report the ideal Zephyr sizes, keeping each target identical across matched alternatives. Save the exact generated graphs and their provenance. Synthetic defects or a sourced device graph are optional, separately labeled extensions.

Use binary arithmetic. For one CSS error sector let H be its syndrome-check matrix and row(G) the stabilizer-equivalent error subspace, with HG^T=0. Given a consistent measured syndrome s and positive weights w, the reference optimization is

$$\min_{e\in\{0,1\}^n}\sum_i w_i e_i\quad\text{subject to}\quad He=s\pmod 2.$$

For independent binary errors, w_i=log((1-p_i)/p_i), with 0<p_i<1/2; uniform weights give minimum-weight decoding. Use perfect syndrome measurements. Logical success requires a syndrome-valid correction whose residual with the unknown true error lies in row(G). The decoder must not receive the true error or its logical class. Treat a missing or syndrome-invalid output as failure. State noise rates, tie conventions and all evaluation inputs.

Choose the mathematical representation, quadratic reduction, preprocessing and embedding methods. The reduction must attain the stated optimum, and every projected ground state must be an optimal feasible correction under a justified tie rule. Each embedded variable must occupy a nonempty connected branch set, disjoint from those of other variables, and each quadratic interaction must have a physical coupler. Include all variables and interactions. Explain energy scaling and chain penalties, and justify programming feasibility conditionally when device limits are unavailable.

Compare at least two defensible formulation/embedding designs, including a reproducible automatic Ocean/minorminer reference, with comparable classical search budgets. Separate the effect of representation from the effect of placement. Report model and physical resource counts, branch-set distributions, programmed coefficient scales, construction/search time, peak memory, search failures and reuse costs across independent syndrome sets. Charge all syndrome-dependent preprocessing, sample recovery and restarts. If preprocessing already solves decoding, report it as a classical decoder followed by answer encoding; a small answer encoding alone is not evidence of an improved annealing formulation.

Validate correctness on exact small cases and independently verify embeddings. Demonstrate faithful handling of periodic checks and logical sectors. Specify a sample-recovery rule and audit it on controlled offline samples, reporting before/after results and costs. Where decoding quality is measured, use matched data and appropriate classical controls, and separate logical failure, syndrome feasibility and optimization accuracy.

Deliver executable reusable code, reproducible instances and models, projection and embedding witnesses, hardware/graph manifests, machine-readable comparisons and an English report explaining the decisive scientific choices and limitations. A supported negative or capacity result is acceptable when demonstrated by controlled comparisons. A single automatic embedding, an unsupported warning or a timeout alone does not answer the question. Do not infer physical QPU performance or quantum advantage from offline proxies, or claim global optimality without a certificate.

Public documentation, literature, open-source libraries and internet access are allowed. No fixed numerical performance threshold, wall-time ceiling or memory ceiling is imposed for this research-design version. Declare the budgets used for comparisons in advance and report actual resource use; do not omit failed attempts. Keep the implementation and experiment choices open and justify your conclusions at the scale actually investigated.

## Expert brief

Keep the objective, inputs, deliverables and success criteria above unchanged. The following principles may help identify scientific errors; they do not prescribe one algorithm.

- Separate the original syndrome-constrained optimization, its quadratic representation and its physical embedding. A parity-check factor graph need not be the quadratic interaction graph. Verify the mathematical reduction before drawing conclusions about placement.
- Logical equivalence requires stabilizer data as well as the syndrome matrix. A syndrome-consistent error can still be a logical failure. Uniform-weight minimum-distance decoding, most-likely individual errors and posterior probability summed over logical classes are distinct objectives. Do not silently substitute one for another.
- Periodic seams are real checks in the input. Removing a seam changes the code. Parameterizing corrections by stabilizers around one representative can omit nontrivial logical classes; justify coverage or any reduction that preserves the original optimum.
- Quadratic penalties, auxiliary encodings and chain couplings compete for usable coefficient scale. Fewer auxiliary bits or shorter chains alone do not define a better hardware model. State sufficient penalty conditions and distinguish energy faithfulness on consistent chains from stronger physical ground-state claims.
- A complete minor-embedding witness contains connected disjoint branch sets for every logical variable and a target-edge witness for every interaction. An isolated logical variable still needs a branch set. Recheck saved witnesses against the exact target graph.
- Ideal Zephyr and a device working graph have different evidential status. Document the generator or exported graph, coefficient limits and coupling-group constraints if used. Avoid copying example limits from documentation as though they applied to an unspecified device.
- Freeze the source model when comparing placement methods, and use comparable embedding search effort when comparing source models. Record successful and unsuccessful searches. Distinguish a failed heuristic search from a proof of non-embeddability.
- A reusable mapping can amortize search over syndromes only when the source interaction structure remains compatible. Charge syndrome-dependent rebuilding and classical decoding costs. If an exact classical decoder supplies the answer before annealing, its cost and scientific role must remain visible.
- Exact small-case solutions can test reduction soundness and ties, but small sanity fixtures cannot establish useful scaling. Choose independent syndrome sets and larger cases that discriminate between alternatives. Keep a numerical gap estimate separate from a certified optimum.
- Chain unembedding, local energy improvement and syndrome repair are different operations. Measure their individual effects and costs, and use matched classical controls where solution quality is assessed. Artificial low-energy samples are useful diagnostics but do not establish a real QPU's excitation or broken-chain distribution.

The scientific work still requires choosing and justifying representations, assessing their coefficient and routing tradeoffs, designing comparisons that distinguish mechanisms, and establishing which recommendation the evidence actually supports. A reasoned recommendation can be a boundary or limitation rather than an improvement, but it requires reproducible evidence.
