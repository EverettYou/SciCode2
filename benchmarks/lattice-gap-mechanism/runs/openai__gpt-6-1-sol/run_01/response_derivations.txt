STRICT ONE-PHYSICAL-ORBITAL RESPONSE AUDIT

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
The physical local Hilbert space is {|0>, |up>, |down>, |up down>}.
All operators below act in that space. P_i=c_(i,down)c_(i,up) is a
composite physical electron-pair operator, not another orbital or an
independent field. The calculations do not establish any one Hamiltonian
as a quantitative fit to all measurements. They do establish why the
ordinary Hubbard model and its simplest pair-hopping limits face strong
constraints, and why a bare-band stiffness bound must be amended when
pair hopping is added.

1. Response functions that a many-body calculation must actually compute
---------------------------------------------------------------------
NMR: R(T)=1/(T1*T) is proportional to
sum_q |A(q)|^2 lim_(omega->omega_N) Im chi_+-(q,omega)/omega.
The supplied measurements.json stores 1/T1, not R(T). The fitted empirical
activation scales are conditional on R=A exp(-E/T): 5.538+/-0.135 D at
half filling, 2.634+/-0.047 D at nu~0.3, and 1.747+/-0.053 D for T<=0.5 D
at nu~0.2. Allowing temperature powers increases their uncertainty.
These are not automatically equal to triplet gaps, U/2, or STS gaps.

For a finite exact-diagonalization cluster, nuclear-frequency response
is a set of delta functions. Inserting a broadening is an additional
approximation; its value and size dependence must be reported. A thermal
occupation curve or static susceptibility is not itself an NMR calculation.

Transport: rho_dc=1/sigma_dc must follow the physical charge-current
operator and current-current Kubo response (including any pair-current
term). Spectral gaps and thermal occupations alone are not conductivity.
The empirical positive-branch rho activation energies are 3.722+/-0.049 D
at half filling and 0.900+/-0.044 D at nu~0.3. The nu~0.2 normal branch
is approximately linear in T. Do not equate all these energies.

2. Strict two-dimensional stiffness obstruction for charge-neutral interactions
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use radian momenta and lattice constant 1. Let
epsilon(k)=-2t(cos kx+cos ky)-4t' cos kx cos ky.
For a physical Cooper-pair phase twist q, an electron hop acquires q/2.
Writing free-energy density F(q)=F(0)+(rho_ab q_a q_b)/2 gives
rho_ab <= (1/(4N)) sum_(k,sigma) n_(k,sigma) d_a d_b epsilon(k).
The paramagnetic response subtracts a positive semidefinite matrix.
For a BKT transition, Tc=(pi/2) sqrt(det rho(Tc^-)). By the trace bound,

 Tc <= (pi/(16N)) sum_(k,sigma) n_(k,sigma)
                          [d_x^2+d_y^2] epsilon(k).

This does not require the state itself to preserve C4: sqrt(det rho)
is at most Tr(rho)/2. Optimize the right side over all Pauli-allowed
occupations 0<=n_(k,sigma)<=1 with mean total electron density n=2nu.
Fill the highest values of C(k)=t(cos kx+cos ky)+4t' cos kx cos ky,
not the lowest epsilon(k). For nu=0.2 and the conditional high-T DOS fit
t=0.9970683 D, t'=0.1953443 D, this yields Tc<=0.2848091 D.
The 256^2, 512^2 and 1024^2 momentum-grid values are 0.28480949,
0.28480910 and 0.28480907 D. The looser maximum-curvature bound is
Tc <= pi*n*(2t+4|t'|)/8 = 0.4359767 D.
Both are below the observed zero-resistance bracket (1.075,1.15] D.

Assumptions matter: the high-T tunneling DOS estimate of t,t' is
conditional on its spectral model; the transition must be strictly 2D
BKT; and the microscopic interactions must carry no uniform charge
current. Onsite U, density interactions and physical spin exchange meet
the last condition. Pair hopping and correlated single-electron hopping
do not. A layered 3D transition also evades this particular 2D bound.

Primary reference: Hazra, Verma and Randeria, Phys. Rev. X 9, 031049 (2019),
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.9.031049 .

3. Pair hopping is a physical one-orbital escape from that bound
---------------------------------------------------------------
For H_J=-J sum_<ij>(P_i^dagger P_j+h.c.), a pair hop acquires the full
phase twist. Its diamagnetic stiffness contribution along x is
J/N sum_x-bonds <P_i^dagger P_j+h.c.>. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds each
coherence by twice the lesser of the double-occupancy and empty-site
probabilities, hence by 2 min(nu,1-nu). For NN square-lattice hopping,
the extra upper bound on Tc is pi*|J|*min(nu,1-nu).
At nu=0.2 it is 0.628319 |J|. Therefore, using the sharper bare bound,
J>1.25765 D is a necessary loose condition to reach Tc>1.075 D.
It is not sufficient and does not predict Tc. Applying the unmodified
bare-band bound to a Penson-Kolb-Hubbard Hamiltonian is incorrect.

4. Repulsive Hubbard/PKH Mott limit and the spin scale
----------------------------------------------------
For the repulsive 10-site charge-gap match U=11.4935251922 D, with
t=0.99706833 D and t'=0.19534428 D, the leading strong-coupling estimates
are J1=4t^2/U=0.34598445 D and J2=4t'^2/U=0.01328031 D; J2/J1=0.03838.
This U is conditional on the finite-cluster canonical gap and prior STS
extraction; it is not a thermodynamic many-body parameter fit.
This is a weakly frustrated square-lattice antiferromagnet, not an
established spin-gapped Mott phase. Over the measured half-filled NMR
range T>=0.4472 D, T already exceeds J1. The spin sector is then dominated
by local moments and exchange-scale dynamics rather than activated
depletion on a 5.5 D scale. A Hubbard charge gap alone does not suppress
the singly occupied local moments. This is a scale/phase mismatch for
the simple Hubbard interpretation, not a theorem about every one-band
interaction.

Take the physical two-site Hamiltonian
H=-t sum_sigma(c_1sigma^dagger c_2sigma+h.c.)
  +U sum_i n_iup n_idown
  +V(n_1-1)(n_2-1)
  -J(P_1^dagger P_2+h.c.).
In its two-electron singlet sector the singly occupied singlet couples
with amplitude -2t to the symmetric doublon-hole state of energy
delta=U-V-J. The relevant block is [[0,-2t],[-2t,delta]]. The triplet
energy is zero, hence the singlet-branch splitting is
Delta_ST=(sqrt(delta^2+16t^2)-delta)/2, reducing to 4t^2/delta for
delta>>t. The script verifies this in the exact physical 16-state Fock
space. This two-site splitting is not a rigorous many-site spin-gap bound.
Pair hopping ~1.3 D in this U=11.4935 D Mott regime modestly changes exchange;
it does not produce a 5.5 D spin scale. When J or V closes delta, the
spin-only expansion is invalid because charge states enter the low-energy
space. Extrapolating 4t^2/delta through that point is unjustified.

5. Exact atomic spin thermodynamics: attraction can suppress moments
-------------------------------------------------------------------
At t=J=V=0 and mu=U/2 the empty and double states have energy zero;
the two single states have energy -U/2. With g*mu_B=1,
chi_s(T)=1/[4T(1+exp(-U/(2T)))].
For U>0 and T<<U, this is Curie 1/(4T). For U<0 it is
exp(-|U|/(2T))/(4T). This establishes the different occupation physics.
It does NOT compute 1/T1: an isolated atomic spin has no relaxation
dynamics. In a weakly mobile paired state, low-energy spin response can
inherit thermal single-fermion activation, but prefactors, coherence
factors, and relaxation mechanisms must be computed. At fixed even total
particle number, breaking a local pair creates two singly occupied sites
and costs |U|; the grand-canonical quasiparticle activation is |U|/2.

6. Attractive PKH strong-coupling projection and the half-filled obstacle
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
For U<0 with singly occupied states well separated, project onto the
physical |0>,|up down> states. b_i=P_i is now a hard-core pair operator;
this projection neither introduces an auxiliary orbital nor enlarges
the physical Hilbert space. The leading NN effective model is

 H_pair=-t_B sum_<ij>(b_i^dagger b_j+h.c.)
        +V_B sum_<ij>(n_Bi-1/2)(n_Bj-1/2)-mu_B sum_i n_Bi,
 t_B=J+2t^2/|U|, V_B=4V+4t^2/|U|, mean n_B=nu.

It is a square-lattice spin-1/2 XXZ model at nu=1/2. For positive t_B,
V_B>2t_B favors checkerboard charge order; |V_B|<2t_B favors pair
superfluidity; V_B<-2t_B favors phase separation. At V_B=2t_B the
NN model has pseudospin SU(2) after a sublattice rotation. Thus the
simple, unfrustrated strong-coupling model has no ordinary uniform,
fully charge-gapped pair-Mott phase at half a pair per primitive cell.
Checkerboard pairing order creates distinct empty-rich and double-rich
sites; uniform measured STS and a single site-resolved NMR environment
strongly disfavor this explanation. Doping the simplest checkerboard
solid also tends to phase separate instead of producing a uniform
supersolid. These are statements about this controlled limit; they are
not a proof covering every intermediate-coupling PKH parameter set.

Primary references:
Robaszkiewicz and Bulka, Phys. Rev. B 59, 6430 (1999),
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9806088 .
Hebert et al., Phys. Rev. B 65, 014513 (2002),
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0105450 .
The former includes mean-field/one-dimensional results; do not present
its full phase diagrams as numerically exact two-dimensional results.

7. A genuine possible route, and what it does not yet explain
------------------------------------------------------------
The half-filled isotropic line occurs at V=J/2 for NN terms. In fact,
this is an exact charge-pseudospin SU(2) line of the full NN fermion
Hamiltonian at mu=U/2, not just of the projected pair model. Define
eta_i^+=(-1)^i P_i^dagger and eta_i^z=(n_i-1)/2. Then each NN bond obeys
-J(P_i^dagger P_j+h.c.)+V(n_i-1)(n_j-1)=2J eta_i dot eta_j
when V=J/2. NN electron hopping and the centered onsite Hubbard term
also preserve the global eta SU(2). These eta operators are composite
physical operators, not additional degrees of freedom.
Consequently large physical pair hopping can coexist with a
strongly suppressed half-filled finite-temperature ordering scale:
continuous pseudospin symmetry forbids finite-T order in strictly 2D,
while doping acts as a pseudospin field and permits a BKT transition.
This is a plausible family for a Tc dome displaced from half filling.
However, the isotropic half-filled ground state has gapless collective
pair/charge modes. It is not a charge-gapped Mott insulator, and the
measured activated dc resistivity E_rho~3.7 D has not been derived from
it. Next-neighbor hopping t' also breaks the simple bipartite
pseudospin symmetry and produces further pair couplings.

The half-filled transport measurements stop at 0.45 D. They do not
exclude superconductivity or electronic ordering below that temperature.
Neither diffraction nor one NMR line alone proves every electronic
symmetry is preserved: some bond or time-reversal order could evade
those probes. Conversely, simply naming such an order does not fit
the four measured observables. A fully symmetric, gapped state with
one odd electron per actual primitive cell faces filling constraints
and would require topological order rather than a trivial product state.

Recommended honest conclusion: the simplest repulsive Hubbard and
unfrustrated attractive-PKH limits fail specific response/phase tests.
Pair hopping is an allowed one-orbital interaction that removes the
strict bare-band Tc obstruction, but no numerical fit to U,J,V and no
thermodynamic phase diagram has been established here. The V~J/2
attractive-PKH family merits an actual many-body charge/spin/current
calculation; it must not be declared a completed quantitative solution.
