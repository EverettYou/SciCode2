FINITE-TEMPERATURE DYNAMICAL SPIN RESPONSE BENCHMARK

System and approximation
H_spin=J1 sum_NN S_i.S_j+J2 sum_NNN S_i.S_j, on the same 10-site
tilted square torus as electron_ed.py. Cyclic NN shifts are 1,-3;
NNN shifts are -2,4. There are 20 bonds of each type. This is the
leading strong-coupling spin-sector projection of the repulsive
single-orbital Hubbard Hamiltonian, with no additional physical orbital.
It is NOT a full finite-T Hubbard calculation: empty/doubly occupied
charge states and higher-order exchange are omitted.

Parameters: t=0.99706833 D, t'=0.19534428 D, U=11.4935251922 D,
J1=4t^2/U=0.3459844542 D, J2=4t'^2/U=0.01328030784 D.
U is the repulsive 10-site canonical charge-gap match in data/hubbard_gap_fits.json.
Both this finite-cluster extraction and the high-T inference of t,t' remain conditional.

Actual Lehmann calculation
All 1024 spin states were diagonalized in all eleven Sz sectors,
whose dimensions are 1,10,45,120,210,252,210,120,45,10,1.
The response includes every local S_i^+ matrix element and averages
over all ten physical sites:

S_loc(omega) = (1/(N Z)) sum_(i,m,n) exp(-E_n/T)
              |<m|S_i^+|n>|^2 L_eta[omega-(E_m-E_n)],
L_eta(x)=eta/[pi*(x^2+eta^2)].

At negligible nuclear frequency, 1/T1 is proportional to S_loc(0),
with an unknown overall hyperfine scale. Therefore the comparison
with the plotted NMR quantity uses S_loc(0)/T. This is a dynamical
Lehmann response, not a static susceptibility or occupation surrogate.
The overall prefactor and convention-dependent factors are absorbed
in a positive fitted amplitude.

Numerical checks
Maximum eigenvector residual: 3.75e-14 in J1 units.
The exact integrated spectral sum rule is
integral S_loc(omega) d omega=<S_i^-S_i^+>=1/2 at zero field.
Using all Lehmann weights, the maximum thermal sum-rule error is
3.33e-16 over the measured temperature grid.
Finite-cluster triplet gap: 0.83680019 J1 = 0.28951986 D.
This finite-cluster gap is not a thermodynamic spin gap.

Fixed physical Hubbard exchange: direct contradiction with the NMR shape
Normalize S_loc(0)/T to its value at T=4D, exactly as for the data.
At the lowest measured half-filled temperature T=0.4472D:

 eta/D       predicted normalized 1/(T1 T)
  0.02                 6.734494
  0.05                 6.868756
  0.10                 6.808953
 measured              0.0000340096

The model increases on cooling over this temperature range while
the data are exponentially suppressed. The discrepancy at this
temperature is about 200,000-fold after common normalization.
Even allowing an arbitrary overall amplitude and fitting the raw
1/T1 data gives chi-square 243.78-244.02 for 17 degrees of freedom.
The high-temperature end T~4D can have appreciable charge corrections
to a spin-only theory, but the enormous low-temperature mismatch is
not caused by omitted thermally excited charge states at T=0.4472D.

Free exchange scale: diagnostic, not a Hubbard fit
Keeping J2/J1 fixed and profiling the amplitude in the raw rate
likelihood gives the following all-temperature fits:

 eta/D     best J1/D       chi-square / degrees of freedom
  0.02       6.2694                   8.7878
  0.05       4.3442                  12.7612
  0.10       1.3483                  14.0586

These finite-width fits still fail. Lorentzian tails from gapped
inelastic transitions produce an artificial nonactivated zero-frequency
floor even at T=0, making extraction of a large gap unreliable.

To separate that broadening artifact from the thermal multiplet scale,
an additional diagnostic keeps only exactly degenerate E_m=E_n
transitions. This is the coefficient of a zero-frequency delta peak,
not a finite physical relaxation rate. It matches the shape only at
J1=8.27834 D, with conditional 68% profile interval [8.07112,8.48966]
and 95% interval [7.87323,8.69945] D; chi-square/dof=0.52956.
The implied finite-cluster triplet gap is 6.92731 D.
Restricting the fit to T<=2D (actual highest retained T=1.6596D)
gives J1=7.83997 D, 68% interval [7.56162,8.12158], and
95% interval [7.29283,8.39990] D; chi-square/dof=0.13988.

Thus even the delta-weight diagnostic needs roughly 23-24 times the
Hubbard exchange inferred from t~D,U=11.4935D. Reinterpreting it via
J1=4t^2/U would give U~0.48D, where the strong-coupling projection is
invalid and which is inconsistent with using a large-U Mott description.
Do not report these profile intervals as microscopic parameter errors.

Interpretation and limits
This is a concrete response-level failure of the simplest repulsive
Hubbard strong-coupling spin sector at the proposed parameters.
It does not rule out direct physical spin exchange, frustration,
symmetry breaking, momentum-dependent hyperfine form factors, or other
single-orbital interactions. No thermodynamic phase diagram is inferred.
No result here turns a phenomenological spectral fit into a many-body
Hamiltonian fit. The finite-T spin response approximation, the full
state count, the hyperfine assumption, and the broadening dependence
must accompany any use of the plot.

Reproduction
Run: python3 response_spin_dynamics.py
Inputs: data/measurements.json and the previous conditional hopping
fit at data/sts_prior_spectral_fit_results.json, plus the conditional
10-site U from data/hubbard_gap_fits.json.
Outputs: response_spin_dynamics.json, .npz, and .png.
The NPZ retains all dimensionless energies, transition energies,
local matrix-element weights, and initial-state indices.
