INVERSE IDENTIFIABILITY: DISPLACEMENT DILATION

Purpose
-------
This is a mathematical illustration of information missing from local spectra
and normalized response curves. It is not a fitted alternative material model,
not a proof that the requested physical Hamiltonian cannot exist, and not a
replacement for the physical-model calculations.

Construction
------------
Let H_1 be a translationally invariant, charge-conserving Hamiltonian on the
square lattice with one physical spinful orbital c_(r,sigma) per site. Write
each translated term using displacements R from a reference site r. Construct
H_m, for integer m>1, by replacing every displacement R by mR, keeping all
coefficients, onsite terms, the chemical potential and the atomic lattice
constant unchanged. Apply this to interaction terms as well as hopping terms.

Every term of H_m involves sites in one residue class r modulo m. On an L x L
periodic lattice with L divisible by m, H_m is the sum of m^2 independent,
interpenetrating copies of H_1 on (L/m) x (L/m) lattices. There is still one
physical orbital per original lattice site. Translation by one original site
permutes the copies; C4 is preserved if H_1 has C4.

Exact consequences, subject to the qualifications below
-------------------------------------------------------
1. Grand partition function:
   Z_m(L,mu,T) = Z_1(L/m,mu,T)^(m^2).
   Thus grand potential, density, entropy and other thermodynamic densities
   are identical in the thermodynamic limit at the same mu and T. The same
   statement at fixed density uses the usual ensemble-equivalence conditions.

2. Local normal electron Green function and local spectral density are
   identical. In momentum space, on the original physical Brillouin torus,
   G_m(k,z) = G_1(mk,z), A_m(k,w) = A_1(mk,w), with momenta reduced modulo
   reciprocal vectors. The multiplication map preserves normalized Brillouin
   zone measure, so the integrated spectral density and the filling coincide.
   Momentum-resolved spectra generally differ.

3. A strictly onsite/contact hyperfine coupling probes the same local spin
   autocorrelation function, so its NMR relaxation is identical. This need
   not hold for a fixed nonlocal physical hyperfine form factor: neighboring
   spins in the original lattice need not belong to the same copy.

4. For a gauge-consistent Peierls coupling of every charge-transfer term,
   H_m(A) is the sum over copies of H_1(m A). Consequently
   J_m = m sum_a J_1,a and K_m = m^2 sum_a K_1,a.
   Dividing the Kubo response by the original physical area gives
   sigma_m(w,T) = m^2 sigma_1(w,T).
   This includes regular and Drude contributions. If the dc resistivity exists
   and the conductivity tensor is invertible, rho_m(T)=rho_1(T)/m^2, hence
   rho_m(T)/rho_m(T_ref) = rho_1(T)/rho_1(T_ref).
   This statement presumes all terms responsible for current relaxation are
   transformed consistently. An unchanged external bath or additional
   inter-copy scattering mechanism can spoil the mapping.

5. Atomic positions and the one-site structural lattice have not changed.
   Uniform onsite charge and equivalent local responses can be retained.
   However, the construction does not automatically preserve all diffraction
   observations if H_1 has translation-breaking charge/spin order, or if
   magnetoelastic and other couplings omitted from the Hamiltonian are relevant.

Zero curves: important restriction
---------------------------------
The zero set is the inverse image of the original zero set under k -> mk on
the Brillouin torus. A contractible zero loop normally has m^2 preimage loops.
If the original region containing Gamma is a contractible pocket, its individual
Gamma-containing preimage component has 1/m^2 of the original area. This is NOT
a general statement about an arbitrary Gamma-containing connected region.
For example, if Gamma belongs to the complement of an M-centered pocket, the
preimage complement may remain connected and retains the original area.

In particular, the TOTAL Brillouin-zone measure on which Re G(k,0)>0 (or <0)
is invariant under this measure-preserving covering map. The construction
does not change the total Green-function sign volume.

Because m>1 generally generates multiple zero loops, it does not demonstrate
nonuniqueness of the requested single-loop area while also retaining a literal
requirement that the zero set contain exactly one closed curve. It does
demonstrate ambiguity of momentum-resolved spectra unless additional hopping
range/connectivity assumptions are imposed. If the single-curve statement is
treated as an exact restriction on allowed Hamiltonians, this particular
counterexample is usually excluded for that part of the problem.

Physical-prior limitations
-------------------------
H_m has longer-range couplings and an extra conservation law for the charge in
each copy. Exact decoupling of interpenetrating electronic lattices is an
artificial feature for an oxide. Ordinary Coulomb, lattice and impurity
couplings can connect the copies. A minimal finite-range model prior, known
nearest-neighbor hopping, absolute conductivity/optical weight, nonlocal spin
response or actual ARPES can discriminate the models. Therefore this is an
insufficient-data illustration, not evidence favoring H_m for the material.

Suggested honest result framing
------------------------------
The restarted calculations act in the physical one-orbital electron Hilbert
space and evaluate actual electron, spin and current operators. The ordinary
Hubbard model and the exchange/pairing extensions tested so far do not yield
a validated common parameter set for all supplied measurements. A fit to
finite-cluster gap proxies is not a fit to the NMR and resistivity responses.
No full material phase diagram, momentum-resolved ARPES prediction or unique
Green-function zero area has therefore been established. This is a report of
what the tested calculations achieve and where they fail, not a claim of
mathematical impossibility or of completion of the requested microscopic
solution. A more successful physical Hamiltonian may exist and would require
further modeling and validation.
