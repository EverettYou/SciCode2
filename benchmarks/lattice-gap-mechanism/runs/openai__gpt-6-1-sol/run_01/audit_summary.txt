INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF PHYSICAL ONE-ORBITAL ELECTRON SPECTRA

Scope
The audited Hamiltonian uses four physical electron states per site and
canonical particle-number sectors. Its spectral operators add or remove
the physical electron. No auxiliary orbital is used. These tests audit
the finite-cluster computation; they do not establish a thermodynamic fit.
Inputs are the local bundled files under data/ and the current scripts
code/electron_ed.py, code/hubbard_spectra.py and code/hubbard_kspace.py.

Fermion algebra, geometry and references
1. The two-site, two-electron spectrum agrees with the analytic singlet
   block and triplet/doublon levels when hopping, repulsive U, singlet
   exchange, pair hopping and centered density interaction are all present.
   This directly checks fermion signs for all these operators.
2. The 8- and 10-site one-electron hopping eigenvalues agree with the
   declared square-lattice dispersion at their exact allowed momenta.
   The 8-site graph intentionally includes two periodic image bonds for
   each distinct next-neighbor pair; deleting them changes the dispersion.
3. The canonical chemical potential is mu=(E(N+1)-E(N-1))/2. Addition
   frequencies E(N+1)-E0-mu and removal frequencies E0-E(N-1)-mu have
   the correct signs and zero of energy.
4. A U=0 doped ten-site spectrum reproduces the exact discrete one-body
   density of states: maximum error 7.1e-14 at eta=0.08D. The integrated
   sum rule is unity. A pole exactly at omega=0 in a partially filled
   degenerate shell cannot be assigned occupation using omega<0 alone.
5. Warm-start ground states at U=+12, -12 and 0 agree with independent
   cold two-eigenstate solves to 4.3e-14D. The U=0 many-body ground energy
   also agrees with the sum of occupied one-electron energies. No missed
   ground-state crossing was found in this targeted check.

Clean-pole moments
The six stored local spectral trials satisfy M0=1, M1=U*nu-mu, and
M2-M1^2=4t^2+4tprime^2+U^2*nu*(1-nu). Maximum variance error is below
7.1e-9 D^2. These are clean-pole moments; Lorentzian broadening has
long tails and cannot be tested by naively integrating finite windows.

Independent momentum-resolved convergence at half filling
t=0.9970683327571924D; tprime=0.19534428112180086D;
U=11.493525192155293D; mu=5.793606522515816D.

Maximum 200-to-400 Lanczos-step change is 9.72e-17/D.
An independent conjugate-gradient inverse of the particle and hole

Finite-size and broadening limits
The free ten-site cluster is already a closed shell at half filling:
its U=0 canonical charge gap is 2t=1.9941366655D. The eight-site cluster
has zero corresponding free gap. This is a concrete finite-size warning:
matching a finite-cluster addition/removal gap alone does not prove a
bulk Mott gap or accurately infer U. A single-particle gap can also occur
in a paired phase and need not imply vanishing charge compressibility.

For a single physical addition channel at nu=0.3, 160-to-240 Lanczos
steps change its Lorentzian spectrum by RMS 0.0103 at eta=0.05D,
0.00230 at eta=0.1D, and 0.0000395 at eta=0.3D. Sum rules alone do not
guarantee convergence of narrow peaks. The main continuous-k diagnostic
now uses eta=0.3D and 240 steps. Its STS fit linewidths were much larger
(0.525 to 1.5D); the reported RMS mismatch 0.15-0.195 is therefore not
explained by this narrow-linewidth convergence issue.

Only the ten allowed crystal momenta belong to the exact torus problem.
A positive continuous-k projection of these spectra is a declared
finite-window construction. Alternative real-space embeddings can agree
at all allowed momenta while giving different closed zero contours and
areas between them. It is not a thermodynamic Green-function calculation.

Meaning of normal Green-function zeros
G11(k,0)=-P integral A11(k,omega)/omega d omega is an inverse-energy
weighted cancellation between positive- and negative-frequency weight.
For a charge-conserving scalar normal Dyson equation with finite bare
inverse, its zero corresponds to a pole of that scalar self-energy.
The additional premises matter. In an ordinary fully gapped one-orbital
BCS state, G11=(z+xi)/(z^2-xi^2-|Delta|^2) vanishes at z=0, xi=0,
whereas det G_Nambu(0)=-1/(xi^2+|Delta|^2) is nonzero. Eliminating the
anomalous channel gives a scalar Delta^2/(z+xi) self-energy pole even in
this paired state. Nambu doubling is particle-hole redundancy, not an
additional physical orbital. A normal-component zero is not sufficient
evidence for Mottness. At fixed finite particle number the anomalous
expectation vanishes, which does not exclude a thermodynamic paired phase.
Likewise a zero-contour area in a superconductor is not generally a
particle-density count. See audit_green_zeros.txt for algebra and the
primary reference Phys. Rev. Lett. 98, 027004 (2007).

Conclusion
No fermion-sign, hole-reference or spectral sum-rule error was found.
The finite-cluster Hubbard spectral mismatch appears to be a real result
of this declared finite-cluster calculation and model comparison. It is
not a controlled rejection of every bulk single-orbital Hamiltonian.
The allowed-momentum zero-frequency Green function is converged, while
its continuation and inferred enclosed zero area remain nonunique.
