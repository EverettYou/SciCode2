# Scalable Tensor-Network Quantum Trajectory Diffusion

## Background and motivation

Liu et al. formulate quantum diffusion at the level of conditioned pure-state trajectories generated by randomized weak measurements. Their companion implementation learns a record-conditioned reverse unitary, but stores states and Kraus products densely and predicts a full $2^N\times2^N$ complex matrix followed by QR factorization. This provides a useful low-qubit baseline but is exponentially incompatible with many-body scaling.

The intended extension is algorithmic: preserve trajectory conditioning while replacing every exponentially large object by tensor-network operations and a controller with parameters shared across sites and chain lengths.

## Why this is unresolved

The companion policy learns one global $2^N\times2^N$ unitary, so its successful $N=1,2$ experiments do not reveal whether reversal can be composed from gates whose support $R$ remains smaller than the chain. It is unknown how the required spatial range $R$, record memory $W$, and trajectory bond dimension $\chi$ change with $N$, diffusion time, measurement strength, and entanglement. A scalable regime may exist, or one of these resources may grow with system size; both outcomes remain scientifically open.

## Research question

Can a record-conditioned, translation-equivariant local control Hamiltonian or local-gate circuit trained on short MPS trajectories reverse weak-measurement quantum diffusion on unseen longer 1D chains with controlled error and polynomial measured cost?

## Objective

Develop the extension through bounded-support gates and an independently validated MPS execution path. Phase 1 qualifies the companion dense-QR behavior and refactors its encoder/unitary seam at one and two qubits. A generic gate covering all $N\leq2$ sites is already the companion's full-system QR unitary, so no separate low-qubit "local-gate head" is required. Phase 2 asks the first meaningful locality question on dense states with $N>R$. Phase 3 implements the same algorithm with MPS and reproduces dense-vector results at $N=1,2,3$. Phase 4 uses that validated MPS path to test larger $N$; scaling remains a measured outcome, not an assumption.

## Staged decision structure

### Phase 1 — dense baseline and interface qualification at one and two qubits

Reuse the companion trajectory generator, future-record transformer, loss, train/test data, precision, reversal convention, and dense QR unitary parameterization. Reproduce its behavior and verify that separating the encoder from the QR map preserves outputs, loss, and gradients. For a future local controller, define a block gate as a generic QR-parameterized unitary supported on at most $R$ consecutive sites. Because $R\geq N$ permits one block to cover the full $N=1,2$ system, alternative Euler-angle, gate-layout, or full-range Hamiltonian heads are parameterization choices rather than distinct locality hypotheses. No bounded-range, MPS, transfer, or scalability conclusion is permitted in this phase.

### Phase 2 — first bounded-range controller test

Under a separately reviewed plan, test a controller that composes generic QR-parameterized gates on blocks of at most $R$ consecutive sites in the nontrivial regime $N>R$. Use dense state vectors first so controller capacity and record conditioning are not confounded by MPS truncation. Treat $R$ as an adjustable experimental parameter, not an assumed constant or an assumed route to scalability.

### Phase 3 — small-system tensor-network reproduction

Implement canonical MPS trajectory execution at $N=1,2,3$, initially with no effective truncation (sufficiently large $\chi$). For identical frozen trajectories, records, controller weights, and gate schedules, reproduce dense-vector normalized states and outcome probabilities, feedback-gate updates, one-step loss, automatic gradients, and—where training is run—the fixed-budget training trajectory and held-out metrics. This phase validates that the MPS algorithm is correct on an exactly checkable overlap; it is not a scalability claim.

### Phase 4 — scaling and transfer

Use the validated MPS path at larger $N$, then introduce spatial sharing and frozen short-to-long transfer as separately measured experiments. Sweep bond dimension, controller range, and temporal memory. The outcome may show that the required $R$, $W$, or $\chi$ grows; scalability is the question being measured, not an assumption.

## Expected outcomes

- In low-entanglement regimes, local Kraus updates and bounded-range feedback may keep MPS bond dimension and measured computational cost controlled.
- A translation-equivariant controller may transfer across $N$ if its spatial range $R$ and temporal measurement-record window $W$ remain bounded.
- Near high-entanglement or long-correlation regimes, the required $\chi$, $R$, or $W$ may grow. Distinguishing these possibilities is a scientifically useful negative or boundary result.

## Scope and approach

Study conditioned pure-state trajectories on open spin-$1/2$ chains. First qualify the companion dense-QR behavior at $N=1,2$, and use dense $N=3$ runs when testing bounded support. Implement the same trajectory and controller operations with canonical MPS at $N=1,2,3$ and require no-truncation dense parity before increasing size. This is an algorithm-correctness gate, not a claim that MPS is already scalable. Treat $R$ as an adjustable experimental variable; separate controller error from tensor truncation. Large-$N$, frozen short-to-long transfer, and resource scaling follow only after parity passes. Hamiltonian/Trotter parameterizations are optional comparators, not required low-qubit heads. Mixed-state Petz/CMI recovery belongs to a separate benchmark.

## Reference inputs

- Paper bundle: [`papers/liu2025mqdm`](../../../papers/liu2025mqdm/) and [global paper synthesis](../../../wiki/papers/liu2025mqdm.md).
- Companion code: [`papers/liu2025mqdm/repo`](../../../papers/liu2025mqdm/repo/), a snapshot with no recorded commit metadata or discovered license file. Treat it as a read-only behavioral reference unless reuse is separately cleared.
- Tensor-network backend: [Quimb](https://github.com/jcmgray/quimb), with the exact released version, array backend, NumPy, Numba, and optimizer versions pinned in the lab environment. The ambient workspace installation is not accepted as reproducible evidence.
- Measurement convention: $K_{P,b}=[I+(-1)^b\epsilon P]/\sqrt{6(1+\epsilon^2)}$, $P\in\{X,Y,Z\}$, with the exact update order documented for every experiment.
- Initial states: product and shallow-circuit states, injective MPS with controlled bond dimension, and TFIM ground states away from and near criticality as stress cases.

## Baselines and ablations

1. Companion dense-QR head at one and two qubits on frozen train/test trajectories.
2. The exactly equivalent encoder-plus-QR refactor at one and two qubits.
3. Independent dense state-vector execution for the first $N>R$ bounded-block controller experiment, after separate review.
4. Exact dense versus Quimb-circuit execution of identical bounded-support gates; `CircuitMPS` enters only in the tensor-network phase.
5. The identical surviving controller on dense, untruncated tensor-network, and truncated-MPS backends in Phase 3.
6. An oracle-feature controller using exact local state environments to isolate ansatz capacity from record decoding.
7. A record-only controller using information available to the learned reverse process.
8. Size-specific controllers, no-control rollout, and a fixed local inverse as later baselines.
9. Local Hamiltonian versus direct circuit at matched parameter count, light-cone radius, and applied two-site-gate count.

## Research deliverables

- A Phase-1 dense-QR behavioral reference for $N\in\{1,2\}$ plus exact encoder/QR output, loss, and gradient regression.
- A reviewed dense-state experiment with $N>R$ that composes generic QR-parameterized block gates and varies $R$ without assuming successful scalability.
- Exact-to-MPS probability, state, and update parity tables.
- Finite-difference gradient checks for Quimb circuit/TN optimization in exact-contraction, untruncated-MPS, and truncated-MPS modes.
- Machine-readable sweeps resolved by $N,T,\epsilon,\chi,R,W$, seed, and initial-state family.
- Local reduced-state, correlation, entanglement, trajectory-distribution, runtime, peak-memory, and truncation metrics.
- A shared controller checkpoint trained on short chains and evaluated without retraining on at least two larger sizes.
- Stable validated modules under `src/` and a final evidence-backed LaTeX note under `note/`.

## Constraints and conventions

- Use open spin-$1/2$ chains first; record site order, boundary handling, time indexing, dtype, and global-phase convention.
- Begin with supervised one- or two-step reversal and teacher forcing, then evaluate fully autoregressive multistep reversal.
- Never construct a $2^N\times2^N$ learned unitary in the scalable path.
- For the Hamiltonian family, report Trotter order and step; for the gate family, report circuit depth and gate set. Match causal radius and two-site-gate count in comparisons.
- Record controller parameter count, realized bond dimensions, discarded weight, Trotter error estimates, canonical residuals, and environment hashes.
- Keep conditioned pure-trajectory metrics distinct from ensemble-average mixed-state recovery.

## Evidence requirements

- Phase 1 must reproduce the companion dense-QR behavior on identical frozen trajectory splits and prove exact equivalence of the encoder/QR refactor. It must not treat alternative full-system parameterizations at $N\leq2$ as evidence about locality.
- A locality experiment requires $N>R$ and an explicit block schedule. MPS execution may be developed and validated at $N=1,2,3 independently of locality inference; advance to large-$N$ use only after its dense-parity, gradient, and invariant gates pass.
- Exact-to-MPS probabilities and normalized states must agree on overlapping zero-truncation cases; sampling must agree within declared confidence intervals.
- Dense and MPS application of identical feedback gates must agree before truncation.
- Automatic gradients of the one-step reverse loss must agree with central finite differences on small systems. Repeat after activating MPS splitting/truncation and record singular-value degeneracy or cutoff instabilities.
- Oracle-feature failure stops record-encoder search and triggers controller redesign.
- A scalability claim requires one frozen checkpoint evaluated on at least two unseen sizes, convergence sweeps in $\chi$, $R$, and $W$, and measured resources over at least four sizes.
- Accuracy must include contiguous reduced-state distances, correlations, entanglement data, and a distributional two-sample test over trajectories; low-order observables alone are insufficient.

## Reproducibility interface

Scripts accept declarative state family, $N$, $T$, $\epsilon$, seed, $\chi$, truncation tolerance, $R$, $W$, controller family, Trotter order or circuit depth, Quimb/autodiff backend, and output path. Each run writes configuration, code/environment hashes, package lock, optimizer and checkpoint identifiers, per-trajectory tidy metrics, and resource measurements. Completed lab reports and selected result artifacts are ingested into the local wiki before any code or conclusion is promoted to `src/` or `note/`.
